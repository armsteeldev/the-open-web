# Identity and privacy

`/api/session` issues a random 256-bit first-party HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie (Secure in production), scoped to `/` for all spaces, lasting one year. Only its SHA-256 digest and last placement time are stored. Clearing or changing cookies creates a new identity; this is not proof of a unique human. A process-local per-network placement budget allows 12 immediate placements and refills one token per second (up to 72 in 60 seconds), in addition to the visitor cooldown. Broad guards also cap total requests at 600 per minute and session/write requests at 180 per minute. This permits several people behind a shared address while bounding rapid cookie rotation. Network addresses are retained only in bounded memory, cleaned periodically and never written to the database; shared networks share these limits. Limits reset when the process restarts and cannot prevent distributed abuse. Cooldown digests are indexed by timestamp and expired rows are deleted in bounded batches every 30 seconds and during placements; rows are retained only as long as needed to enforce the cooldown, with cleanup progressing over subsequent batches after a large backlog. No fingerprinting, names, analytics or third-party scripts are used. No public profiles exist. Future optional names must be independent of the secret cookie. The cookie is a bearer identifier: never put it in logs, URLs or broadcasts. Server request logging is disabled; reverse-proxy/access logs must also be configured deliberately.
